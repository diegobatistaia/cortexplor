Petition to Reinstate Jensen Huang at Nvidia Tops 170,000 Signatures

Elon Musk and other tech executives publicly backed the push to bring the ousted CEO back, according to TechCrunch and CNBC.

Newsroom Cortexplor Checked against 2 sources

An online petition called "Stand with Jensen," calling for Jensen Huang's reinstatement as Nvidia's CEO, grew from more than 115,000 signatures on Saturday to nearly 180,000 by Monday, according to TechCrunch and CNBC.

The surge came with public backing from prominent tech figures. Tesla and SpaceX CEO Elon Musk posted on X that he had signed the petition. Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan and Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney also confirmed their support, both outlets reported.

Huang resigned as Nvidia's president, CEO and board member on Thursday, following reports of extramarital relationships, including one with a woman U.S. authorities suspect of ties to Chinese intelligence, as Cortexplor <a href="https://cortexplor.com/en/jensen-huang-resigns-nvidia-ceo-scandal/">reported</a>.

From internal pleas to a public petition

The push for Huang's return began inside Nvidia itself: employees left messages in an internal suggestion box and on corporate messaging channels asking for his comeback, as Cortexplor also <a href="https://cortexplor.com/en/nvidia-employees-demand-huang-return/">reported</a>. The online petition extended that appeal beyond Nvidia's walls, drawing in shareholders and figures from across the tech industry.

TechCrunch reported that, as of Saturday, the petition page listed 5,965 current Nvidia employees, 1,108 former employees and 63,063 shareholders among its signers. The petition's text, per the outlet, states that signers are "concerned that Jensen was removed without meaningful due process" and asks the board to "bring him back while an independent investigation establishes the facts."

Nvidia is keeping CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO while the board searches for both internal and external candidates for the permanent role, according to CNBC and TechCrunch. Both outlets also confirm the board is continuing an independent investigation into the conduct that led to Huang's resignation.

An Nvidia spokesperson told CNBC the company is "in the process of selecting a search firm for the Nvidia CEO role" and has "not formally engaged any potential candidates," calling speculation about individual names premature.

CNBC also lists Shopify CEO Tobias Lütke, former Uber CEO Travis Kalanick and Hugging Face CEO Julien Chaumond among the signatories, all of whom publicly confirmed their signatures on X. The outlet found dozens of other signers identifying themselves as current or former Nvidia employees, though it noted the petition site does not verify self-reported affiliations.

Any potential return still faces hurdles. Besides the ongoing independent investigation, Huang had already agreed to testify before the U.S. Congress about his relationship with the woman suspected of espionage, as Cortexplor <a href="https://cortexplor.com/en/jensen-huang-agrees-testify-congress/">reported</a>. The crisis has also weighed on Nvidia's market value since the first allegation against Huang became public, as Cortexplor <a href="https://cortexplor.com/en/nvidia-shares-fall-jensen-huang-affair/">reported</a> previously.

TechCrunch also notes that Huang's abrupt departure is widely expected to leave a noticeable gap in AI industry funding, given his personal role steering Nvidia's investments across the sector. Neither outlet, though, reports any sign that Nvidia's board is reconsidering its decision to accept Huang's resignation, even as the campaign for his return keeps gathering public support.

In practice

  • What's new: the petition for Huang's return moved beyond an internal employee movement, growing from 115,000 to nearly 180,000 signatures in days, with public backing from figures like Elon Musk.
  • When: the petition gained momentum between Saturday and Monday, days after Huang's resignation was announced on Thursday.
  • Who's involved: Nvidia shareholders, former and current employees, plus tech executives from other companies who signed publicly.
  • What changes: Nvidia's board, which had already accepted Huang's resignation and named Colette Kress interim CEO, now faces public pressure from outside the company as well.
  • What's missing: according to TechCrunch and CNBC, the board is still running an independent investigation into Huang's conduct and continues searching for internal and external candidates for the permanent role.
