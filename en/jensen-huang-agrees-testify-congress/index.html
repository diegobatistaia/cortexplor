Huang agreed to testify to Congress hours before leaving Nvidia

The ousted CEO accepted a closed-door hearing after lawmakers threatened a subpoena, according to Engadget and TechCrunch.

Newsroom Cortexplor Checked against 2 sources

Jensen Huang agreed to testify before the US Congress in a closed-door hearing on Wednesday, hours before Nvidia announced his resignation as CEO on Thursday, according to Engadget. The former executive agreed to appear after lawmakers threatened him with a subpoena.

The information came from US House representatives. House Foreign Affairs Committee Chairman Brian Mast, a Republican, said the hearing is expected to take place before Thanksgiving, on November 26, according to Engadget.

TechCrunch confirms the move in broader terms: Republican committee chairs had already been summoning Huang to testify in closed-door sessions over the security ramifications of the case. The outlet raises a question no source answers yet: whether Democrats, who take over key committees in January, will also call him for a public session.

According to Engadget, the hearing will bring together the House Foreign Affairs Committee with the Intelligence and China-focused committees. "Jensen and I have been very at odds, as some of you already know, on export controls, but Nvidia is a very important company to the United States of America," Mast said, according to the outlet. "I don't want to see anything hurt in Nvidia, but he has some very serious questions to answer."

The summons is tied to Huang's relationship with Alice Huang, a businesswoman US officials believe may be a Chinese intelligence asset — a case Cortexplor has already <a href="https://cortexplor.com/en/nvidia-board-investigation-jensen-huang-china-spy/">covered</a>. Lawmakers want to determine whether the relationship put sensitive information about Nvidia's AI chip supply chain to China at risk.

The US Department of Justice is investigating Alice Huang over whether entities associated with her attempted to smuggle Super Micro Computer products into China, according to reporting cited by Engadget. The department is also said to be leading a probe into Megaspeed, her former Singapore-based employer, over alleged smuggling of US-made chips into China in defiance of export restrictions; the company said it would cooperate with the investigation, per the same outlet.

In Singapore, the Ministry of Home Affairs said on Thursday that Alice Huang has been barred from re-entering the country, after police determined she knowingly offered inaccurate information to downplay her role at Megaspeed, according to Engadget. Officials said there was insufficient evidence to establish further offenses tied to the company's inaccurate declarations, and the investigation into Megaspeed and six other individuals remains open.

Even though the hearing is closed to the public, Mast expects it to be filmed and transcribed, according to Bloomberg reporting cited by Engadget. That means the substance of the testimony could become public later, even if the session itself stays private.

The case adds to other pressure points on the former executive: Nvidia had already opened an internal investigation into the relationship, the FBI confirmed its own inquiry, and shareholders sued the company over allegedly misleading statements, as Cortexplor has reported. Huang left Nvidia one day after agreeing to testify, following the disclosure of a second affair, this time with an Nvidia executive.

In practice

  • What's new: Jensen Huang agreed to testify before Congress in a closed-door session, under threat of subpoena, hours before leaving Nvidia, according to Engadget and TechCrunch.
  • When: the hearing is expected before November 26, according to House Foreign Affairs Chairman Brian Mast, cited by Engadget.
  • Who's affected: Nvidia and Huang himself, already out of the role, plus the wider Washington debate over security in the AI chip supply chain to China.
  • What changes: even though the session is closed, it is expected to be filmed and transcribed, which could make part of the content public later, according to Bloomberg via Engadget.
