Anthropic Shares Sink on Day Two, Dragging Nasdaq Lower

The Claude maker fell back toward its IPO price as chipmakers and Asian and European markets slid too, according to the Guardian and CNBC.

Newsroom Cortexplor Checked against 2 sources

Anthropic shares fell 9% on Friday, November 20, closing at $88.30, near the $83 price set in the company's IPO, according to the Guardian. It was the AI company's second day as a Nasdaq-listed stock.

The previous day, the stock had closed up 17% in its trading debut, after briefly spiking nearly 40% when trading opened, according to the Guardian and CNBC.

The selloff spreads across Wall Street and Asia

Anthropic's drop came alongside a broader pullback on Wall Street. The Nasdaq Composite erased all of its 2026 gains, closing Friday down 5% from its January 1 value, while the Dow Jones and the S&P 500 both entered correction territory, according to the Guardian. CNBC describes the same picture, with Nasdaq futures back in the red and underperforming both the Dow and the S&P before the opening bell.

Chipmaker Nvidia, one of the biggest backers of AI infrastructure buildouts, fell 14% on Friday, according to the Guardian; CNBC reports the same move, with Nvidia and rival AMD both lower in premarket trading that day.

The selloff reached Asia as well. Japan's SoftBank, one of the world's largest AI investors, had its worst trading day in more than two years, down 14.5% in Tokyo, according to the Guardian. CNBC independently reports the same fact, tying SoftBank's slide to the selloff sweeping the Asian tech sector.

Nvidia's slide had an added driver: California served the chipmaker with a subpoena as part of an investigation into whether its advanced semiconductors ended up in China, according to the Guardian. In Britain, the FTSE 100 fell 5.1% over the week, its biggest weekly drop since the start of the Iran war, the same outlet reports.

The flight from risk assets also hit cryptocurrencies and precious metals. Bitcoin fell to a four-month low, dropping below $60,000, according to the Guardian; CNBC independently confirms the cryptocurrency's four-month low. Gold slid below $3,800 an ounce, its lowest level since September 2025, according to CNBC, while the dollar climbed to a 19-month high amid fears of US interest-rate hikes, the Guardian reports.

Doubts over AI investment returns

Capital.com senior market analyst Daniela Hathorn told the Guardian that Anthropic's IPO showed demand for AI exposure remains strong, but that wasn't enough to offset concerns about elevated valuations, profitability and the sustainability of the industry's investment spending. She said market sentiment has shifted from buying the dip to questioning whether the correction still has further to run.

Amodei defends the $900 billion in commitments

In an exclusive interview with CNBC after ringing the Nasdaq opening bell, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei defended the company's AI infrastructure commitments, which total $900 billion. According to CNBC, he said that given the cash on the company's balance sheet and the revenue it already generates each year, those commitments "don't seem so large" once put in context.

IG chief market analyst Chris Beauchamp told the Guardian that Anthropic could be "draining market liquidity," as investors sell shares in other companies to raise cash to buy stock in the newly listed firm.

In practice

  • Who is affected: investors in Anthropic and AI-linked companies, including Nvidia, SoftBank, and memory-chip makers, according to the Guardian.
  • What changed: shares fell back near the $83 IPO price after closing their debut day 17% above it, according to the Guardian and CNBC.
  • Since when: the drop happened on Friday, November 20, the stock's second trading day on the Nasdaq.
  • Context: Anthropic had debuted the previous Thursday valued above $2 trillion, as <a href="https://cortexplor.com/en/anthropic-nasdaq-debut-2-trillion/">Cortexplor reported</a>.

CNBC also reports that gold fell the same week, dropping below $3,800 an ounce for the first time since September 2025, as part of a broader shift away from assets tied to the recent tech and AI rally.
