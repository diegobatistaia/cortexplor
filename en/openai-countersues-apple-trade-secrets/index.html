OpenAI Countersues Apple Over Alleged Trade Secret Theft

The filing accuses Apple of pressuring hardware suppliers and using a rehired employee to access OpenAI's device roadmap.

Newsroom Cortexplor Checked against 2 sources

OpenAI filed a countersuit against Apple on Friday, accusing the iPhone maker of stealing trade secrets and pressuring hardware suppliers against working with OpenAI on future devices, according to the Guardian and Engadget. The filing was made in federal court in northern California.

The countersuit follows a lawsuit Apple filed against OpenAI in July, accusing it of using former employees to obtain Apple's hardware secrets. Both outlets report that Apple's suit names Tang Tan and Chang Liu, who left Apple to join io Products, the AI device startup led by former design chief Jony Ive that OpenAI acquired in 2025.

Tan spent 24 years at Apple, where he was vice president of product design for the iPhone and Apple Watch, before joining the group that launched io Products, according to the Guardian. Apple previously called ChatGPT "rotten to its core," Engadget reported, after OpenAI acquired io.

A rehired employee and access to OpenAI's roadmap

According to the Guardian and Engadget, OpenAI claims Apple rehired a former OpenAI employee who, during six months at the company, had access to information about an unreleased device and OpenAI's hardware product roadmap. OpenAI says that employee carried that information back to Apple.

"Given the timing, it now appears that stealing those documents may have been his reason for joining OpenAI in the first place," OpenAI said in the filing, according to the Guardian. Apple denies using the rehired employee to access OpenAI's confidential information and denies trying to suppress competition in AI device manufacturing, the newspaper reported. OpenAI and attorneys for Tan and Liu did not immediately respond to a request for comment on the new filing, according to the Guardian.

Two companies racing into AI hardware

The dispute reflects a broader contest over AI hardware. Both outlets report that OpenAI is developing AI-enabled personal electronic devices that could compete directly with Apple. OpenAI alleges, according to the Guardian and Engadget, that Apple is trying to compete unfairly, including by encouraging employees to keep corporate files on personal devices and accounts — a practice OpenAI says lets Apple monitor and complicate departures to rival companies.

Apple called OpenAI's countersuit "a transparent attempt to distract from the substance of Apple's lawsuit," according to a statement cited by the Guardian. Engadget reported that Apple had not yet commented on the new filing by the time of its story.

A dispute months in the making

Tension between the two companies has built up over the year. Engadget reports that Apple's original suit alleges former employees secretly downloaded dozens of confidential files on unreleased hardware products, engineering presentations and technical specifications. OpenAI and the named former employees deny the allegations, both outlets report, arguing Apple's case relies on unauthorized monitoring of Liu's personal messages after he left the company.

Both outlets note the litigation runs alongside an existing partnership: Apple and OpenAI worked together to bring ChatGPT to the iPhone, an arrangement that continues even as the two companies sue each other in federal court.

In practice

  • The dispute now covers two separate countersuits: Apple sued OpenAI in July; OpenAI countersued Apple on Friday, according to the Guardian and Engadget.
  • OpenAI alleges trade secret theft and pressure on hardware suppliers; Apple denies the claims and calls the countersuit a distraction from its own case, according to the Guardian.
  • Neither suit had a ruling as of this story's publication; both are proceeding in federal court in California.
  • Who is affected: employees moving between the two companies — OpenAI says roughly 400 people have already left Apple for OpenAI, according to the Guardian — and the hardware suppliers that work with both companies.
