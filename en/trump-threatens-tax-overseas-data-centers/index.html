Trump Threatens 'Tremendous Tax' on US Firms' Overseas Data Centers

At a White House press conference, the president vowed to stop US companies from building data centers in other countries and warned of a heavy tax penalty if they do, according to CNBC and The Verge.

Newsroom Cortexplor Checked against 2 sources

US President Donald Trump said Friday that American companies will be barred from building data centers in other countries and will face a "tremendous tax" if they do so anyway, according to CNBC and The Verge. He made the announcement at a White House press conference, and according to both outlets, the move came as news to his own staff.

"We're not going to allow our companies to build data centers outside of our country," Trump said, according to The Verge. He warned that if the US does not keep the infrastructure at home, "China will take them all," the outlet reported. The remarks came a day after Trump met with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin at a trilateral summit in China, according to CNBC and The Verge.

"We're going to fight very hard for the data centers," Trump said before announcing the new policy, according to CNBC. He argued the US has an edge in hosting the infrastructure: "We have a lot of places that want them badly, and they're great places. They have energy, and they have everything. They're tremendous assets," he said, according to the same outlet.

His own team had no warning

Trump acknowledged he had not briefed his staff before making the announcement. "I don't know if that's a breaking story. I haven't even said that to my people. But I'm telling them now, they're here," he said, according to CNBC and The Verge. Neither outlet reported how the tax would be calculated, what rate it would carry, or when it would take effect — The Verge noted that Trump did not share many details behind the plan.

A comparison to the chip industry

Trump compared the data center buildout to semiconductor manufacturing, which shifted overseas over past decades. "They allowed that to happen with chips and everything else, and it was very destructive to our country. It's where we started going behind," he said, according to The Verge. He also used the term "SI," his preferred shorthand for "super intelligence" (the most advanced stage of artificial intelligence), a term CNBC reported that Chinese President Xi Jinping told him he also favors.

Negotiate, don't block, Trump tells communities

At the same press conference, Trump told communities hosting data centers to negotiate benefits rather than block the projects. "Whoever wins SI wins. And you ought to take advantage of the data centers. Make them pay you. Make them pay your community. But take advantage. Don't shut them off," he said, according to The Verge. He added that if one state turns a project down, "there's always somebody else in this country" willing to take it — the alternative his new tax is meant to discourage is that investment moving outside the United States instead.

Domestic pushback is already underway

The threat lands as US states and cities impose new restrictions on data center construction at home, according to The Verge. A CNBC poll, the All-America Economic Survey, found that 64% of registered voters oppose having an AI data center within 10 miles of their home, versus just 13% who would support one.

CNBC reported that if Trump follows through, his administration will have to contend with the fact that tech giants including Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta and Oracle have already built data centers in other countries to serve international customers, in some cases to meet local regulatory requirements.

The Verge noted that Trump's promise could be hard to deliver on: a new Democratic-majority Congress is set to be sworn in within a couple of months, and even some of the president's own allies already recognize that the data center buildout has become an unpopular issue across the political spectrum, according to the outlet.

In practice

  • Trump made the announcement Friday at a White House press conference, with no effective date set.
  • He did not specify how the "tremendous tax" would be calculated or what rate it would carry, according to The Verge.
  • The threat targets US companies that build or plan to build data centers outside the United States.
  • A CNBC poll found 64% of registered US voters oppose an AI data center within 10 miles of their home; 13% would support one.
