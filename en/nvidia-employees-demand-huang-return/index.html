Nvidia employees push for Jensen Huang's return as CEO

Internal messages cite a lack of transparency over Huang's abrupt resignation, Wired's reporting shows, as relayed by Canaltech and Tecmundo.

Newsroom Cortexplor Checked against 2 sources

Nvidia employees have used internal company channels to ask for Jensen Huang's return as chief executive, days after his resignation, according to a report by Wired that was relayed by Brazilian outlets Canaltech and Tecmundo.

The appeals surfaced in an internal suggestion box and in company messaging tools, both outlets report; Tecmundo adds that some messages also arrived by email. The requests are directed mainly at Nvidia's board, which accepted Huang's resignation and would have to decide on any reinstatement, according to Tecmundo.

Huang stepped down as Nvidia's president, CEO and director on Thursday, after admitting in a letter to employees that he had been unfaithful to his wife, Lori Huang. The admission followed reports of two extramarital relationships, including one with Alice Huang, whom U.S. authorities suspect of spying for China, as Cortexplor <a href="https://cortexplor.com/en/jensen-huang-resigns-nvidia-ceo-scandal/">reported</a>.

The Alice Huang affair wasn't the only one reported: a separate report described a second extramarital relationship between Huang and Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of healthcare, who reported directly to him, prompting Nvidia's board to meet and weigh his removal, as Cortexplor <a href="https://cortexplor.com/en/nvidia-board-weighs-ousting-huang-second-affair/">also reported</a>.

That same board, which had been weighing Huang's removal, is the one that accepted his resignation and named chief financial officer Colette Kress as interim CEO, alongside Harvey Jones as the board's first-ever chair, as Cortexplor previously reported. According to Tecmundo, the board is made up mostly of longtime investors and former Nvidia employees, and it's the body the return requests are aimed at.

The crisis also hit Nvidia's market value, which has lost billions of dollars in market capitalization since the first affair became public, as Cortexplor <a href="https://cortexplor.com/en/jensen-huang-resigns-nvidia-ceo-scandal/">previously reported</a>.

According to Canaltech and Tecmundo, part of the workforce expected the board's independent investigation to conclude before any change in leadership. That didn't happen: Huang resigned before the inquiry's findings were released.

What the internal messages say

Canaltech cites two examples of messages employees left in the internal suggestion box. One reads: "If you believe in the mission, bring the boss back." Another says: "we are all human and we make mistakes. Jensen did so many good things and he deserves a second chance." According to the outlet, more than 1,600 employees left comments on the internal channel.

Tecmundo describes the messages as mostly anonymous and supportive of Huang, while also acknowledging his mistake. The outlet says Nvidia had not publicly commented on the episode by the time Wired's report came out.

According to Tecmundo, the push to keep Huang in the role reflects his standing inside the company: he co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and had led it for 33 years, a tenure the outlet describes as a key reason some employees see his removal as premature.

A return by Huang is seen as unlikely, according to Tecmundo, which notes the board is unlikely to reverse a decision it just made. The executive, who co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and led the company for 33 years, still has to testify before the U.S. Congress about his relationship with Alice Huang, as Cortexplor <a href="https://cortexplor.com/en/jensen-huang-agrees-testify-congress/">also reported</a>.

In practice

  • What's new: Nvidia employees used internal channels to ask for Jensen Huang's return as CEO, according to Wired, relayed by Canaltech and Tecmundo.
  • When: the messages emerged in the days following Huang's resignation, announced on Thursday.
  • Who's involved: Nvidia employees, who are directing their requests at the company's board of directors.

  • What's still unclear: according to Tecmundo, a reinstatement is seen as unlikely, and there's no sign the board is reconsidering its decision.
