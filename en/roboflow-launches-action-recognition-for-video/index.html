Roboflow adds action recognition to its vision platform

The computer vision platform now tags the start and end of each action in a video clip, the company says.

Newsroom Cortexplor Based on 1 source

Roboflow announced support for action recognition on its computer vision platform, according to the company's own announcement. The feature trains models that watch a video clip and return a timeline of detected actions, each with a start and end time.

Until now, Roboflow's platform was built mainly around object detection — identifying what appears in a single image frame. The new feature extends that reading across time: the model analyzes a sequence of frames to understand what happens throughout a video, rather than what exists in one isolated frame, the company says.

Roboflow says two models power the feature: Cosmos 3 Edge and V-JEPA 2.1. Both look at a video in 16-second windows and label each moment inside a window with one of the actions a user defines. On longer videos, the window slides along the clip, and the results are merged into a single timeline, according to the company.

Roboflow tested both models on three public datasets and says V-JEPA 2.1 was more accurate on every one of them. Cosmos 3 Edge, according to the company, tends to place the start and end of each action more precisely — something that can matter for teams that need the exact timing of each step, not just confirmation that it happened.

In an internal test on a gear train assembly dataset — 82 videos recorded by 41 people, split into 29 for training, 6 for validation and 6 for testing — Roboflow says V-JEPA 2.1 reached 87.3% accuracy after roughly 8 hours of training, versus 80% for Cosmos 3 Edge after about 12 hours.

The company cites monitoring production lines and robot operations as a typical use case: whether an operator completed every step in order, how long each step took, or whether a robot finished its sequence or had to retry a move. Roboflow says running the model over a week of existing footage produces a timestamped record of every step in every cycle.

Roboflow also offers a Python software development kit, installed with pip install -U inference-sdk, to send video to the base model or to a version a customer has trained. For live cameras, the company says the feature can be added to a workflow inside the platform, or run on a customer's own servers through Roboflow Inference.

To build a model, a user creates an "Action Recognition" project on the platform, uploads videos, and marks time ranges for each action directly on a timeline instead of drawing boxes around objects. Roboflow also offers an auto-labeling option: a user names the actions and describes when each one happens, then reviews the segments the system finds before training starts, according to the company.

In practice

  • What it is: a video action recognition feature that tags the start and end of each step in a recorded process, instead of only detecting objects in a single frame.
  • Who can use it and where: available on the Roboflow platform (app.roboflow.com); the Cosmos 3 Edge model can be tried through the API before any training, according to the company.
  • Since when: Roboflow announced the feature on November 11, 2026, and it is already live on the platform.
  • Pricing: Roboflow Training bills 2 credits per GPU hour; the company says its gear train test cost about 16 credits using V-JEPA 2.1, which is in beta and limited to paid plans.
  • What changes from before: the platform previously recognized only objects in isolated frames; it now reads sequences of actions over time, according to the company.
  • Who is affected: industrial quality-control, logistics and robotics teams that already use video to monitor their processes.

To train a model outside Roboflow's own cloud, the company recommends a GPU with 24 gigabytes of memory or more and says the process can take hours to days of supervision.
