Jensen Huang says he'd be honored to return as Nvidia CEO

In his first public remarks since resigning, Huang said he's open to a comeback, as Nvidia's board creates a new export oversight committee.

Newsroom Cortexplor Checked against 5 sources

Jensen Huang, who resigned as Nvidia's CEO on November 12, made his first public comments since leaving the company on Monday, November 16. In a written statement, he said he would be open to returning as chief executive if Nvidia's board decided that was in the company's best interest, according to the Guardian, CNBC, The Verge and TechCrunch.

Huang said in the statement that, if the board decided his return served shareholders, employees, the company and “America,” he would be “honored to answer these calls” and come back as the company's chief executive. According to TechCrunch, Huang posted the statement on the social network X; the Guardian reports it was also shared with the Wall Street Journal and CNBC.

In the same text, Huang acknowledged making “serious mistakes” in his personal life and said he takes “complete” ownership of them. CNBC reports he said he is committed to cooperating with the board's independent investigation into his conduct.

Huang resigned after reports surfaced that he had extramarital relationships, including one with a former executive at a Nvidia business partner suspected of acting on behalf of Chinese intelligence, as <a href="https://cortexplor.com/en/jensen-huang-resigns-nvidia-ceo-scandal/">Cortexplor reported</a> at the time.

Despite the statement, Nvidia's board is not currently weighing Huang's reinstatement, according to a person familiar with the matter cited by CNBC. The Guardian cites a similar conclusion from the Financial Times, and The Verge attributes to CNN the report that the board is “not considering” bringing Huang back.

Nvidia is scheduled to report its fiscal third-quarter earnings on Tuesday, according to CNBC and the Guardian. That call will give investors and analysts their first chance to question interim CEO Colette Kress directly about how the company plans to move forward without its founder in the role.

A new export-control committee

While Huang waits for an answer, Nvidia's board has created an internal panel to oversee the company's compliance with US export rules, called the National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee, according to Engadget. Suzanne Nora Johnson, a former Goldman Sachs vice chair and a recent addition to Nvidia's board, will lead it.

According to Engadget, Nvidia's existing national security and global trade compliance teams will now report to the new committee and to interim CEO Colette Kress. The panel, which also includes board members Stephen Neal and John Dabiri, is expected to meet at least once a quarter. Its creation was also mentioned independently by the Guardian and TechCrunch.

Harvey Jones, chairman of Nvidia's board, said the company is committed to working with government and industry partners to balance national security with continued innovation, according to Engadget. The outlet notes Nvidia did not say whether the committee can halt sales on its own or only flag concerns to the board.

The new structure arrives as scrutiny grows over how Nvidia handled export compliance in the months before Huang's departure, Engadget reports. Huang, who founded Nvidia in 1993 and ran it for 33 years according to CNBC, is still scheduled to testify before the US Congress on December 1, as <a href="https://cortexplor.com/en/jensen-huang-agrees-testify-congress/">Cortexplor has reported</a>.
