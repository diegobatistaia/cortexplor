Anthropic shares close below IPO price, but analysts stay bullish

The stock fell 6.5% on Tuesday to trade below its $83 IPO price, even as three banks started coverage with buy ratings.

Newsroom Cortexplor Checked against 2 sources

Anthropic shares closed down 6.5% on Tuesday, falling below the AI company's $83 IPO price, according to CNBC. TechCrunch reported the stock trading as low as $76 a share at midday, down nearly 12% from Monday's close.

The maker of Claude opened at $111.50 on the Nasdaq last week after raising $81.3 billion in the largest public offering of all time, CNBC reports. The stock has been sinking steadily since then. CNBC attributes part of the decline to broader volatility across the AI trade, driven by concerns over the feasibility of a multitrillion-dollar infrastructure buildout.

Why trading below the IPO price matters

TechCrunch notes that falling below the IPO price is often seen as a negative signal, since underwriters typically price offerings at a discount to their expected trading value. The outlet reports that venture capitalists and other observers are watching Anthropic's performance closely because it could serve as a barometer for how public market investors value the sector — and a continued slide could pressure private startup valuations and weaken IPO prospects for other AI companies still weighing a public listing.

CNBC confirms that Anthropic's offering was oversubscribed and that roughly half of the institutional investors who placed orders for shares were left empty-handed. Even so, as the broader market wavers, those same firms don't appear to be rushing to buy shares now that they are trading below the IPO price, the outlet reports.

A broader sell-off in AI stocks

Anthropic wasn't the only AI stock falling on Tuesday. CNBC reports that Nvidia, Advanced Micro Devices and Intel, as well as neoclouds CoreWeave, IREN and Nebius, all fell during intraday trading. TechCrunch notes Nvidia dropped more than 7% by midday, giving back some of the 30%-plus gain it posted Monday after Jensen Huang was reinstated as the chipmaker's CEO. CNBC adds that the broader market was still absorbing that reversal, which had briefly lifted Nvidia's shares by more than 30% before Tuesday's pullback.

Three banks start coverage with buy ratings

Despite the drop, CNBC reports that three banks initiated coverage of Anthropic in recent days with bullish calls. Canaccord Genuity started coverage with a buy rating and a $135 price target, saying the company is building "one of the most important enterprise technology franchises of the AI era." Wedbush initiated coverage with an outperform rating and a $120 target, and Oppenheimer did the same with a $150 target, citing the company's compute efficiency and its multi-chip supplier strategy.

According to CNBC, Wedbush's analysts wrote that "while there's going to continue to be many ups and downs, and winners and losers as this ecosystem gets built, in our view there is no real question that it does get built over time," adding that Anthropic "is a strong play on that theme." Oppenheimer, for its part, said that "while competition and compute commitments remain risks, we believe Anthropic can translate its leadership into durable growth and operating leverage," the same outlet reports. CNBC notes that Wedbush issued its rating on Monday, while Canaccord Genuity and Oppenheimer both initiated coverage on Tuesday, the same day the stock slipped below its IPO price.

  • Anthropic shares closed at $83 on Tuesday, down 6.5% and below the IPO price; intraday, they traded as low as $76, according to TechCrunch.
  • The stock opened at $111.50 the previous week after Anthropic raised $81.3 billion in the largest public offering on record, per CNBC.
