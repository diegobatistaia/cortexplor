Nvidia Board Rules Out Jensen Huang's Return as Record Quarter Lands

Chairman Harvey Jones confirmed during Nvidia's first earnings call since Huang's ouster that the board will not reinstate him, as revenue jumped 103% year over year.

Newsroom Cortexplor Checked against 3 sources

Nvidia reported revenue of $115.8 billion for the fiscal third quarter ended October 25, up 103% from a year earlier and 20% from the previous quarter, in its first earnings report since founder and former CEO Jensen Huang's departure, according to the Guardian and Engadget. The figure beat Wall Street's expectation of roughly $109 billion. Earnings per share came in at $2.68, also above the $2.47 estimate, according to the Guardian.

During Tuesday's call with investors, Nvidia board chair Harvey Jones said directly that the board does not plan to bring Huang back. According to Engadget and TechCrunch, Jones said: "On November 12th, our board of directors announced that after disclosure of personal behavior inconsistent with the company's code of conduct, standards, and cultural norms, Jensen Huang voluntarily resigned his positions as president, CEO, and board member. The Nvidia board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO."

The statement answered a request Huang himself had made hours earlier. On X, he wrote that if the board decided his return was in the best interest of shareholders, employees, the company and the country, he would be "honored" to answer those calls and come back as CEO, according to the Guardian, Engadget and TechCrunch.

Chief financial officer Colette Kress, named interim CEO last week, led the call. The Guardian and Engadget reported she described the business as running on an "as usual" basis and said Nvidia would take care of employees through the transition. Engadget and TechCrunch reported that Jones said the board is weighing both internal and external candidates for a permanent CEO and will not rush the process. Engadget reported that Jones said the industry is living through a moment when advanced semiconductor technology and national security dynamics are driving major shifts in the market, and that the next CEO will need to navigate those waters "very carefully."

Nvidia's data center segment, which generates most of its revenue, more than doubled to $107.6 billion, Engadget reported. Shares rose 5% in after-hours trading following the announcement, according to the Guardian.

The results land days after Nvidia shed more than 10% of its market value, with its valuation falling to $3.7 trillion from an October peak of nearly $6 trillion, the Guardian reported. The slide followed Huang's November 12 resignation after reports of extramarital affairs, including one with a woman described by US officials as a possible Chinese intelligence asset. The Guardian, citing the Wall Street Journal, also reported that the US Securities and Exchange Commission is investigating Nvidia's business in China.

As CEO, Huang had about 60 direct reports, giving him control over most of the company's daily functions, the Guardian reported. Nvidia now controls roughly 90% of the market for AI chips, and leading AI companies, including OpenAI, Anthropic and Google, rely on its hardware to train their models, according to the same outlet.

Jones also pointed to Kress's 13 years at Nvidia, saying she has been "at the center of this company's extraordinary growth," the Guardian reported. Closing the call, according to Engadget, Kress summed up the company's position: "Our momentum is real. Our demand is accelerating. Our supply is growing. Our customers and our partners are with us, and employees are committed."

In practice

  • Who decided: Nvidia's board, unanimously, as stated on the record by chair Harvey Jones during a public earnings call.
  • What's new: this is the first official, on-record confirmation of the board's position — before this, resistance to reinstating Huang had only been reported through anonymous sources.
  • Leadership now: Colette Kress remains interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent replacement, considering both internal and external candidates with no set timeline.
  • The numbers: $115.8 billion in quarterly revenue, up 103% year over year, above analyst expectations.
