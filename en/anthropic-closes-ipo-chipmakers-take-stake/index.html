Anthropic Closes $119.5B IPO as Chipmakers Take a Stake

The company confirmed underwriters fully exercised their option and disclosed a private placement with AMD, Broadcom, Google, Microsoft, Nvidia and others, according to an official statement.

Newsroom Cortexplor Checked against 2 sources

Anthropic closed its initial public offering on November 20 after underwriters fully exercised their option to buy additional shares, bringing the total Class A shares sold to 1,127,000,000 at $83.00 each, the company said in an official statement.

Of that total, 1,103,351,354 shares came from Anthropic itself and 23,648,646 from existing shareholders who sold part of their stake; Anthropic received no proceeds from that second group, according to the statement. Gross proceeds from the offering were approximately $93.5 billion, of which about $91.6 billion went to Anthropic, the company said.

In the same statement, Anthropic disclosed the closing of a parallel private placement: the sale of 313,253,007 shares of a separate class, Class N, to seven buyers — Advanced Micro Devices (AMD), Broadcom, Google, Micron Technology, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC Partners — also at $83.00 per share. That deal raised approximately $26.0 billion, according to the company.

Combining the public offering and the private placement, Anthropic says it raised roughly $119.5 billion in gross proceeds before underwriting discounts and other offering expenses. Class A shares began trading on the Nasdaq on November 19 under the ticker "ANT"; Class N shares, sold only to the seven buyers, do not trade on an exchange.

Why the chipmakers' stake matters

The Class N buyer list groups hardware makers and cloud providers that also compete with or supply infrastructure to Anthropic's rivals, including OpenAI. By buying shares of a class separate from the publicly traded stock, these seven companies gain a direct financial stake in Anthropic's performance without changing the composition of the shares that trade freely on the open market, according to the terms described in the company's statement.

The public offering had been priced on November 18 at $83.00 per share, with a 30-day option for the underwriting banks to buy up to 147,000,000 additional shares. That option was exercised in full, setting the final size of the deal announced now. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley acted as lead book-running managers, with nine other banks as book-running managers and seven more as co-managers, according to Anthropic.

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission had declared the offering's registration statement effective on November 18, a condition required before shares could be sold to the public, according to the company. Anthropic says the offering could only be made through the final prospectus, the document that details the deal's terms and risks to investors who bought into the sale.

Cortexplor had already reported that Anthropic's stock surged 34% on its November 19 debut, reaching a $2 trillion market value, and that it fell back the following session, dragging down other markets. This Friday's formal closing finalizes the deal's numbers, including the previously undisclosed private placement with the seven hardware and cloud companies.

In practice

  • Who can trade it and where: Class A shares, under the ticker "ANT" on the Nasdaq, have been open to any public market investor since November 19; Class N shares, bought by AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC Partners, do not trade on an exchange, according to Anthropic.
  • Since when: the offering was priced on November 18, shares began trading on the 19th, and the formal closing, with underwriters' option fully exercised, took place on November 20.
  • How much it cost: $83.00 per share in both deals, with gross proceeds of $93.5 billion from the public offering and $26.0 billion from the private placement, totaling $119.5 billion, according to the company.
  • What changes: the closing sets the final size of the capital raised and makes AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC Partners direct Anthropic shareholders through the Class N stake.
