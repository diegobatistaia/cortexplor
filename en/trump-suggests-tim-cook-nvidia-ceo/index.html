Trump suggests Apple's Tim Cook as Nvidia's next CEO

The US president posted the pick on Truth Social hours after Jensen Huang's resignation, according to TechCrunch and Hipertextual.

Newsroom Cortexplor Checked against 2 sources

US President Donald Trump suggested former Apple CEO Tim Cook as a replacement for Jensen Huang at the helm of Nvidia. Trump made the pick in a Truth Social post hours after Huang's resignation from the company on Thursday, according to TechCrunch and Hipertextual.

According to both outlets, Trump wrote that "only one man" could take over the role Huang left behind: Tim Cook. The president described the move as "one legend replacing another legend."

Cook stepped down as Apple's CEO on September 1, after 15 years running the company, and moved into the role of executive chairman of its board, according to TechCrunch. He remains engaged with policymakers and Apple's international relationships, including with China, the outlet reports. John Ternus took over as Apple's CEO.

Trump was not the only one to suggest Cook for the job. Jeffrey Sonnenfeld, of the Yale School of Management, made the same case in an op-ed published by Time on Wednesday, arguing Cook has "an unparalleled track record of succeeding a bigger-than-life founder" — a reference to his succession of Steve Jobs at Apple — according to TechCrunch.

Trump did not explain why he picked Cook for the job, and TechCrunch notes it is unclear whether the president genuinely backs the idea or simply floated something that crossed his mind. The outlet adds that the Apple executive has kept a good relationship with the US president across his terms, much as Huang did.

Hipertextual notes that Cook is not a product-focused executive the way Huang was — a profile the outlet says was crucial to Nvidia's growth under a single CEO across its 33-year history.

The pick comes amid another source of turbulence for the former executive: hours before leaving Nvidia, Huang had agreed to testify before the US Congress in a closed-door hearing over security questions tied to his relationship with Alice Huang, as Cortexplor has also <a href="https://cortexplor.com/en/jensen-huang-agrees-testify-congress/">reported</a>.

Trump's suggestion carries no weight over Nvidia's official succession process. Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress has already taken over as interim CEO, and the board has named Harvey C. Jones, former CEO of Synopsys, as the company's first-ever board chair while it searches for a permanent replacement, weighing both internal and external candidates, as Cortexplor has already <a href="https://cortexplor.com/en/jensen-huang-resigns-nvidia-ceo-scandal/">reported</a>.

Hipertextual notes that, as of its report, there was no sign Cook would be willing to leave his current role at Apple for such a high-profile position elsewhere, particularly given the turbulent circumstances of Huang's exit.

Sonnenfeld's op-ed explicitly points to Cook's record steering Apple after taking over from founder Steve Jobs as the reason he could do the same for Nvidia, according to TechCrunch. Whether Trump's post reflects a serious push or an offhand remark, it adds Apple's former chief to a succession conversation Nvidia itself has not publicly tied to any single name. Neither Trump's post nor Sonnenfeld's op-ed addresses whether Cook has any background in semiconductors or in the accelerated-computing business that made Nvidia the dominant supplier of AI chips, a gap TechCrunch does not fill either.

In practice

  • What's new: Trump publicly suggested Tim Cook as Nvidia's next CEO in a Truth Social post, according to TechCrunch and Hipertextual.
  • When: the post came hours after Jensen Huang's resignation, announced Thursday.
  • Who's affected: Nvidia, Apple and the AI chip market, which is watching the race to replace Huang.
  • What changes: nothing officially — Trump's suggestion does not alter Nvidia's ongoing succession process, still led on an interim basis by Colette Kress.
  • What's missing: there is no indication Tim Cook has responded to the proposal, or that Nvidia's board considers him a candidate alongside the internal and external names it says it is reviewing, according to Hipertextual.
