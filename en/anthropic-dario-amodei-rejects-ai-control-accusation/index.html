Anthropic's Amodei calls AI human-control accusation 'absurd'

Dario Amodei pushed back on X against the idea that Anthropic wants humanity to hand over decision-making power to AI.

Newsroom Cortexplor Checked against 2 sources

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published a lengthy post on X on Wednesday rejecting the idea that his company believes humanity should surrender free will to artificial intelligence systems. According to TechCrunch and the Spanish outlet Computer Hoy, Amodei called the suggestion "absurd."

"We should never sacrifice legitimacy or self-determination at the altar of efficiency or security," Amodei wrote, according to both outlets. He added that the same argument he makes against autocratic rule applies, in his view, to handing society's governance over to AI.

Answering rivals' criticism

TechCrunch reports that the statement comes after executives at rival companies have publicly argued for preserving human decision-making power as AI systems grow more capable. The outlet cites OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, who said earlier this month he wants to make sure people retain "agency over the future," and Meta chief AI officer Alexandr Wang, who wrote in an X essay that society "should not be okay with a 'handover'" to AI.

Computer Hoy reports that Amodei's post was a direct response to an Axios story published the same Wednesday, which described AI executives increasingly distancing themselves from a view the outlet attributed to Anthropic: that humanity should cede some decision-making power to superintelligent AI systems.

Where Amodei draws the line

Both outlets report that Amodei distinguishes between automating specific tasks — such as driving a car, designing a cancer drug or writing software end to end — and a broader surrender of human free will. He argues that letting AI handle bigger, more complete tasks "is economically valuable" and, in his view, can save lives.

On recursive self-improvement, AI that can improve itself without human assistance, Amodei acknowledged that "we may want limits on this process." Per his own post, cited by both TechCrunch and Computer Hoy, Anthropic "does not expect it to be safe" to let Claude build successive frontier models fully autonomously, without meaningful human supervision or understanding.

The statement lands as Anthropic itself leans further on AI for its own research. According to TechCrunch, the company said in June that Claude authored more than 80% of the code merged into its own codebase, and an independent assessment published this month by AI safety nonprofit METR found Anthropic's models are improving at roughly twice their historical rate, driven mainly by AI-assisted research.

TechCrunch quotes more of Wang's essay: "it is hard to imagine a world where humanity can maintain control over fully unconstrained recursive self-improvement," he wrote, adding that the industry "must not only make progress in the extremely urgent areas of safety, alignment, and security, but we have to start thinking through how we progress thoughtfully."

Accusing rivals of 'vibes-based' positioning

Amodei also accused competitors of engaging in "vibes-based commercial and political positioning," repeating phrases like "human control and agency" or "AI should just be a tool" without changing how their own products approach automation. According to TechCrunch, he wrote that every company claims to understand the risks of automation and to offer safeguards, without any material difference in how their products are oriented.

Computer Hoy notes the post ran longer than Amodei's typical statements and had already drawn nearly 7 million views on X, a sign of how much attention the debate over human control of AI is getting among industry executives.

In practice

  • Amodei's post went up on X on Wednesday, November 25, and had already reached close to 7 million views, per Computer Hoy.
  • He is publicly denying that Anthropic wants to surrender human free will to AI, responding to criticism that, per TechCrunch, came from executives at rivals including OpenAI and Meta.
  • He draws a line between automating specific tasks (driving, diagnosing, coding) and ceding society's governance to AI.
  • Anthropic maintains, in Amodei's own words, that it does not expect it to be safe to let Claude build successive frontier models without meaningful human supervision.
