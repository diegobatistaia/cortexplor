Anthropic Debuts on Nasdaq, Market Cap Tops $2 Trillion Mark

Shares opened 34% above the IPO price in the largest offering by funds raised on record, according to CNBC and Fortune.

Newsroom Cortexplor Checked against 2 sources

Anthropic, the maker of Claude, began trading on the Nasdaq Thursday under the ticker "ANT." Shares opened at $111.50, up 34% from the IPO price, pushing the company's market capitalization to roughly $2 trillion, according to CNBC and Fortune.

The offering had been priced Wednesday at $83 a share, above the $70-to-$78 range the company had marketed. Anthropic and selling shareholders raised $81.3 billion by selling about 980 million shares, a figure the company upsized just before pricing, CNBC reported.

By funds raised, it is the largest initial public offering on record, surpassing the $75 billion SpaceX raised in June, according to CNBC. At the IPO price, Anthropic's fully diluted valuation stood at about $1.5 trillion, per CNBC and Fortune. SpaceX's own IPO valuation, at $1.77 trillion, remains higher than Anthropic's, Fortune noted, even though Anthropic raised more cash.

Revenue surges, accounting loss widens

Anthropic generated $33.24 billion in revenue for the nine months ended September 30, up roughly 1,200% from the same period a year earlier, CNBC and Fortune reported. Over the same stretch, the company posted a $104 billion net loss, which both outlets attributed mostly to changes in the fair value of convertible notes and derivative liabilities rather than to the underlying business.

A debut against a jittery market

The listing lands just over a week after Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia's CEO amid a scandal that had already put AI-linked stocks on edge, according to CNBC and Fortune. On the day Anthropic surged, the Nasdaq and the Dow Jones Industrial Average were both trading lower, Fortune reported.

In a roadshow video cited by CNBC, chief executive Dario Amodei said the company aims to "create an age of miracles" with the technology, provided its risks are addressed as carefully as its benefits. In a statement issued Thursday and cited by Fortune, Amodei said the moment "is bigger than any one company, or any one country," and that Anthropic "will not bend to either business or political pressure."

Part of the path to this point was already known: in November, Anthropic had trimmed its valuation target to $1.4 trillion and locked in $26 billion in investment commitments from partners including Google, Microsoft, Nvidia and AMD, as Cortexplor reported. Thursday's pricing, above the marketed range, cleared that revised target.

The tenth tech company worth $1 trillion

With the debut, Anthropic joins a select group of U.S. publicly traded companies valued above $1 trillion, according to CNBC and Fortune. CNBC counts Anthropic as the group's tenth member, a club that had shrunk after AMD and Micron slipped below that mark on recent stock declines. Rival OpenAI, CNBC noted, remains private and is not expected to go public before 2027.

The debut also drew political pushback. According to CNBC, Democratic Senators Mark Warner and Chris Van Hollen and Representative Pramila Jayapal publicly criticized the lack of regulation over the AI industry given the size of the offering, while more than 100 protesters gathered outside Anthropic's San Francisco offices to call for a ban on AI systems designing their own successors without human oversight, a practice researchers call recursive self-improvement, CNBC reported.
