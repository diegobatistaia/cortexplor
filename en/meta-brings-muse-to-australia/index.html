Meta Expands Its Personal AI Agent Muse to Australia Market

Local partners in shopping, real estate and fitness come with the rollout, as Muse reaches its third confirmed market, Meta says.

Newsroom Cortexplor Based on 1 source

Meta has expanded its personal AI agent Muse to Australia, adding local partners for shopping, real estate, fitness and entertainment, according to a company announcement published on November 17.

Muse launched in the United States in September, with access through a dedicated app, a website and WhatsApp, as <a href="https://cortexplor.com/en/meta-launches-muse-personal-ai-agent/">Cortexplor reported</a> at the time. In October, the agent reached Singapore, as <a href="https://cortexplor.com/en/meta-brings-muse-to-singapore/">Cortexplor also reported</a>; Australia is the third market Meta has confirmed.

Local partners and what the agent can do

The first connector partners in the country, according to Meta, include Canva, Domain, Kogan, MECCA, Officeworks, realestate.com.au, SWEAT, ShopBack and Ticketek. The company says each connector's features roll out progressively, with some only becoming available after the local launch.

According to Meta, users can ask Muse to design visuals with Canva, browse property listings through Domain and realestate.com.au, or work toward fitness goals with SWEAT. The agent can also help buy electronics through Kogan, beauty products through MECCA, office supplies through Officeworks and tickets through Ticketek, while comparing prices and earning cashback on shopping and travel through ShopBack.

Real-time voice and custom Spaces

Meta says real-time voice conversations with Muse are now available on mobile, web and Mac, activated by tapping the phone icon in the top-right corner of the screen. The company has also rolled out Spaces, custom environments users create inside Muse by describing an idea; Meta itself lists a shopping hub, a school dashboard, a wedding planner and a trip planner as examples.

How the Muse Secure VM works

Muse runs on an architecture Meta calls the Muse Secure VM: a dedicated cloud computer that stores the data and credentials for connected services, keeping passwords and payment details out of the agent's direct reach, according to the company. Meta says users choose what Muse can access, and the agent checks with the person before sensitive actions, such as sending an email or making a purchase.

In its earlier launches, in the United States and in Singapore, Meta described this same security architecture, backed by a layer called Sentinel and by a Stripe-built payment system that generates a single-use card number for each purchase, as <a href="https://cortexplor.com/en/meta-brings-muse-to-singapore/">Cortexplor reported</a> at the time. The Australia announcement does not repeat those technical details, but it also does not indicate any change to the architecture used in the other two countries.

Will Easton, Meta's managing director for Australia and New Zealand, said the timing suits the holiday season, since Muse could help Australians find gifts and plan trips while people stay in control of which purchases the agent is allowed to make, according to the company. He added that as local experiences roll out, the familiar services connected to Muse should make the agent more useful in everyday life.

In practice

  • Who can use it and where: users in Australia, through the iOS and Android app, the muse.ai website and a Mac app, according to Meta; access through the company's AI glasses still has no date.
  • Since when: from Meta's announcement, published on November 17, 2026.
  • How much it costs: free for most uses, with subscription plans for people who want to do more, according to the company.
  • What changes: Muse reaches its third confirmed market after the United States and Singapore, now with its own shopping, real estate and fitness partners in Australia.
  • Who is affected: Australian users who already use the partner apps, such as Canva, Domain, Kogan and Officeworks.

Meta did not say whether or when Muse will reach other markets, including Brazil or other parts of Latin America, nor did it give a date for the features it describes as available only "after launch" in the country.
