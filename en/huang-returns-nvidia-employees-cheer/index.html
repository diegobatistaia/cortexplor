Jensen Huang Returns to Nvidia HQ to Cheering Employees

A petition calling for his reinstatement topped 200,000 signatures, according to TechCrunch and The Verge.

Newsroom Cortexplor Checked against 7 sources

Jensen Huang walked back into Nvidia's headquarters on Monday to hundreds of employees cheering and chanting his name across three floors, according to videos shared with CNBC and TechCrunch. Addressing the crowd, Huang thanked them "for the second chance" and said he would "be a better CEO the second time around."

The scene came hours after Nvidia's board announced it had reinstated Huang as CEO, president and board director, unanimously reversing a decision made six days earlier not to bring him back. Huang had resigned on November 12th following reports of extramarital relationships, including one with businesswoman Alice Huang, whom U.S. intelligence suspects of ties to China.

CNBC reported that cupcakes reading "Jensen is back" circulated inside the office. A person inside the company, who asked not to be named because they weren't authorized to speak publicly, told the network there was a mix of excitement and frustration among staff over how the past two weeks had unfolded.

The reinstatement also marks a new chapter for "Stand with Jensen," the online petition pushing for his return. More than 200,000 people had signed it by Monday, including Tesla and SpaceX CEO Elon Musk, according to TechCrunch and The Verge.

The Altman precedent

ZDNet and SiliconANGLE both drew a parallel to Sam Altman's brief ouster and reinstatement at OpenAI in November 2023, when the board removed him and brought him back five days later under pressure from investors and staff. Both outlets framed the two episodes as evidence that boards struggle to replace founders who concentrate technical and political leadership inside AI companies.

A different side of the executive

The celebration inside Nvidia contrasts with another portrait of Huang raised by current and former employees to The New York Times, cited by TechCrunch: based on court records and more than a dozen interviews, the executive reportedly used humiliation as a management tool and was sharply critical of subordinates over the years.

Board under internal pressure

A former Nvidia employee who spent more than 15 years at the company told CNBC the reversal is "embarrassing for the board" and that some directors could be replaced. Just a week earlier, the board had said publicly, during its quarterly earnings call, that it did not intend to bring Huang back.

Nvidia shares also reacted to Huang's return, recovering close to $1 trillion in market value during Monday's trading session. The company's independent investigation into the Alice Huang matter remains ongoing, with Huang's cooperation.

The rally followed a week in which the Nasdaq Composite had slid 10%, its worst week since 2020, amid investor doubts about the valuations of artificial intelligence companies, according to CNBC and Wired. Monday's gains also spread to other AI-linked stocks, including Alphabet, Meta and AMD, CNBC reported.

Nvidia board chair Harvey Jones remains in his role, according to The Verge and Mobile Time. Kress returns solely to the chief financial officer duties she held before stepping in as interim CEO, a position she took on November 12th when Huang resigned.

The episode is also a reminder of how concentrated Nvidia's influence has become across the AI industry, through its supply deals and investments in other companies, Wired noted. Huang co-founded the chipmaker three decades ago, according to The Verge and SiliconANGLE, and it now sits at the center of the infrastructure race powering generative AI.

In practice

  • Huang resumed the roles of CEO, president and board director at Nvidia, effective immediately, on Monday.
  • The petition backing his return passed 200,000 signatures, including Elon Musk's, according to TechCrunch and The Verge.
  • The independent investigation into the Alice Huang matter continues despite the reinstatement.
  • CFO Colette Kress stepped down as interim CEO and returns solely to her finance role.
