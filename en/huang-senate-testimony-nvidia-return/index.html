Huang Set to Testify to Senate After Nvidia Reinstatement

The Senate Banking Committee scheduled a Dec. 3 hearing on chip exports as Nvidia's stock recovered nearly $1 trillion in value, according to CNBC and TechCrunch.

Newsroom Cortexplor Checked against 4 sources

Jensen Huang, reinstated as Nvidia's chief executive on Monday, is scheduled to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on December 3, according to TechCrunch. The hearing covers export controls on AI chips to China, one of the issues at the center of the crisis that forced him out of the role on November 12.

CNBC confirms, in broader terms, that Huang is expected to appear before Congress twice in December to answer questions about China's access to advanced Nvidia chips and about US export rules. Per TechCrunch, the second hearing, in the House of Representatives, is set for December 1, before the Foreign Affairs Committee, the Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party, and the Intelligence Committee.

On the day Huang returned to the role, Nvidia's stock jumped about 31%, according to CNBC, a rally that restored close to $1 trillion in market value the company had lost since announcing his resignation, per CNBC and Business Insider. By Tuesday morning, however, shares were down roughly 5% in premarket trading, CNBC reported.

Huang had stepped down 11 days earlier after reports surfaced of extramarital relationships, including one with a Megaspeed executive the US government suspects of working as a Chinese spy. Nvidia's board said it reversed course after hearing from "senior levels of the United States government."

President Donald Trump confirmed he personally intervened in the decision. In a Truth Social post reproduced by TechCrunch, he said "the United States Government has no reason to believe that Jensen's return to NVIDIA poses any risk to National Security" and said the attorney general found no evidence the executive had engaged in unlawful conduct.

Investigations into the case are continuing, CNBC reported. California's Department of Justice is examining whether advanced chips or confidential Nvidia information reached China and whether the company misled investors. CNBC also cited Reuters reporting that the US Securities and Exchange Commission is looking into the company's accounting practices. Lawmakers from both parties renewed calls for a broader investigation, according to CNBC.

Analysts quoted by CNBC are split on what comes next for the stock. William Blair's Sebastien Naji said Huang's return "clears some of the uncertainty," but argued recent events exposed "deeper questions around corporate governance, leadership succession, and regulatory oversight" with lasting implications for Nvidia's competitive position. StoneX's Cody Acree called the outcome of the board's internal investigation the "key risk" that remains. Questar Capital Partners' Richard Reyle told CNBC the scrutiny will make it harder for Nvidia - which he called "the central bank of the AI trade" - to keep up its pace of dealmaking. Bernstein's Stacy Rasgon said the board now faces "legitimate questions as to their judgment and process," and noted that Democrats taking control of congressional committees in January could keep Huang and Nvidia in the political "crosshairs."

CNBC's Jim Cramer raised his price target for Nvidia shares to $199, equal to roughly 12 times next fiscal year's earnings estimate, up from the roughly 10 times multiple the stock traded at, according to FactSet data cited by the network. Cramer argued Huang's reinstatement removes an overhang that had weighed on the stock, though that target reflects his own opinion, not a market consensus.

In practice

  • Hearing dates: House committees on December 1 and the Senate Banking Committee on December 3, per TechCrunch.
  • What's at stake: AI chip export controls to China and the alleged Chinese spy affair, according to TechCrunch and CNBC.
  • Market impact: Nvidia stock rose about 31% the day Huang returned and recovered close to $1 trillion in value, per CNBC and Business Insider, before giving back ground in the next premarket session.
  • Ongoing probes: California's Department of Justice and, per Reuters as cited by CNBC, the SEC.
  • Context: Huang's reinstatement as CEO, 11 days after resigning, was reported by Cortexplor on November 23.
