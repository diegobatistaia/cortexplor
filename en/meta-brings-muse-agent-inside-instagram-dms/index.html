Meta Brings Its Muse AI Agent Inside Instagram's DMs

The assistant now answers directly in chat threads and sorts saved posts, a move that is already lifting Instagram's shopping and ad business.

Newsroom Cortexplor Checked against 4 sources

Meta built its personal AI agent Muse directly into Instagram direct messages on Tuesday, according to TechCrunch and Engadget. The move removes the need to switch between Instagram and the separate Muse app to talk to the assistant.

According to TechCrunch, the feature began rolling out Tuesday to iOS and Android users in the United States, Canada, Mexico, Japan and Singapore. Conversations started on Instagram then show up as a side chat inside the main Muse app, the company said.

Instagram also started prompting users on Tuesday to let Muse organize posts and Reels they have saved, according to TechCrunch and Engadget. That automatic prompt is rolling out first in the United States and Canada.

Among the examples TechCrunch cited: asking Muse to turn a saved recipe video into a grocery list, combine saved travel content into a trip itinerary, or suggest outfits based on saved fashion Reels.

Engadget notes the strategy mirrors how Meta grew Threads, which now has more than 500 million users, by pushing a new product into apps that already have billions of users. Muse itself passed 10 million users last month, according to Engadget.

According to Engadget, Zuckerberg has said he wants Muse to eventually reach billions of people and has predicted the agent could one day generate more revenue for Meta than its existing suite of apps. Instagram still runs a separate, generic chatbot called Meta AI, which answers questions and is not going away, an Instagram spokesperson told Engadget; Muse in DMs is meant to work more like an assistant that completes tasks.

According to Meta, Muse works through connectors: once a user links accounts such as email, a calendar or a shopping app, the agent can handle tasks on its own, like filling out a form or negotiating a refund, but it always asks for approval before important actions, such as sending an email or completing a purchase.

In practice

  • Who can use it: Instagram users who have linked their account to Muse, in the US, Canada, Mexico, Japan and Singapore for DM chat, according to TechCrunch.
  • Since when: starting November 24, 2026.
  • How it works: send Muse a message right inside Instagram, or share a post with it, with no need to open a separate app.
  • What changes: before, users had to switch between Instagram and the Muse app; now the assistant replies directly in the message inbox, TechCrunch said.
  • Limits: inside Instagram, Muse is still more limited than in the full Muse app or website, according to Engadget.

The integration also has a commercial angle. According to CNBC, which cited people familiar with the matter, about 20% of daily Instagram users save at least one post per day, and many of those saved items are shoppable products or ads, making Muse's organizing feature a path toward more purchases inside the app.

CNBC cited data from analytics firm Forter Intelligence showing Muse accounted for 51% of all agentic shopping orders in the week ending November 22, ahead of xAI's Grok at 24% and OpenAI's ChatGPT at 22%, with the remaining 3% split between Google and startup Instinct. On an earnings call cited by CNBC, Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg said 25% of Muse users already shop through the agent, with an average purchase above $200.

Separately, Meta said in a statement the same day that Muse also became available in the UK, with access to local retail partners including Marks & Spencer, Sainsbury's and ASOS, according to the company. Meta said UK users can also connect Muse to brands such as Currys, Argos, Trainline, easyJet and JD Sports, with PayPal support coming soon.
